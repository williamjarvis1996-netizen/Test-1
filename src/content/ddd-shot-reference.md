# Dinner, Diamonds and Death — Shot Reference Guide

## Film Overview

**Title**: Dinner, Diamonds and Death (DDD)
**Script**: Draft 16 — Written by Sean Nwachukwu & Adam Sorrell, Directed & Script Edited by Will Jarvis
**Genre**: Non-linear psychological thriller / crime drama
**Structure**: 35 scenes, roughly 18 minutes. The narrative crosscuts between four storylines that converge: Alexis (hitwoman), Gary (addict thief), Yvonne (drug courier), and Hans (crime boss). A chess motif — white pawn, white bishop, white rook, black queen, checkmate — punctuates the basement scenes.

## Key Locations

- **Canary Wharf Apartment**: Clean, white-walled, minimalist high-rise flat with floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the Isle of Dogs / Docklands skyline. Night scenes show city lights through the glass. Cool blue-grey colour temperature.
- **Angelo's Restaurant Basement**: Underground dining space with bold graffiti-covered walls (cartoon characters, eyes, geometric patterns), a green neon "BASEMENT LIQUOR" sign, mismatched wooden furniture, shisha pipes on shelves. Warm amber/green tones.
- **Hans's Basement**: Raw, oppressive space — exposed brick, bare hanging lightbulb, concrete floor, exposed pipes and wiring on the ceiling. A chess set and a wooden easel or frame are positioned centrally. Desaturated, occasionally washed in deep red.
- **Gary's Flat**: Cramped, cluttered bedsit with a dark sofa, posters on the walls, a kitchenette visible through doorways. Warm, dingy amber lighting. Feels lived-in and chaotic.
- **Davina's House (Exterior)**: Suburban residential street at night — hedgerows, parked cars, terraced/semi-detached houses, sodium streetlights. Desaturated, naturalistic.
- **Balcony (Canary Wharf)**: Open-air high-rise balcony with the Docklands skyline spread behind. Night. Used for the confrontation between Alexis and Yvonne.
- **Bathroom**: Black-and-white chequered tile floor, white panelled cabinets, a frosted window — classic British bathroom. The chequered floor mirrors the chess motif.

## Characters (Visual Identification)

- **Alexis**: Young woman, light hair pulled back, lean build. Wears practical dark clothing — leather/quilted jacket over a light t-shirt. Carries a pistol. Composed, watchful expression.
- **Gary**: Young man, long curly dark hair, light beard. Wears a paisley/patterned open shirt over a white vest, chain necklace. Emotive, volatile energy.
- **Clarence**: Gary's twin brother — same actor, same long curly hair and beard. Distinguished from Gary by context: he is always bound/restrained in Hans's basement, wearing darker, dirtier clothing.
- **Hans**: Older man, bearded, dark skin, heavyset. Wears a dark waistcoat over a shirt. Carries himself with quiet authority. Always seen in the basement or the restaurant.
- **Angelo**: Older man, grey/white hair and beard, large build. Wears a white dress shirt, open collar. Restaurateur — always seen in or near the restaurant basement.
- **Yvonne**: Young woman, dark hair, wears a hooded top or bandana/headscarf. Drug courier who becomes a hostage. Seen on suburban streets and in the apartment.
- **Davina**: Young woman, Alexis's ill sister. Seen in a hooded dark jacket approaching her suburban house at night.
- **Bodyguards**: Two masked figures in skull masks and dark clothing. They work for Hans and accompany Gary on the heist.

## Shot-by-Shot Reference

### Shot 01
- **Timecode**: 00:00:11
- **Scene**: Scene 1 — INT. CANARY WHARF APARTMENT - NIGHT (Opening)
- **Character(s)**: Alexis
- **Location**: Canary Wharf apartment — hallway/entrance area
- **Framing**: Medium close-up, slightly off-centre. Alexis fills the right half of frame, the apartment corridor recedes behind her left shoulder.
- **Lighting**: Cool, desaturated grey-blue. Flat overhead lighting from the apartment's ceiling fixtures creates minimal shadows. The white walls glow faintly.
- **Colour palette**: Steel grey, muted white, the faintest warmth on her skin.
- **Action**: Alexis enters the apartment with her weapon drawn — a pistol held close to her body, pointed forward. Her expression is focused and alert, scanning the space. This is the opening image of the film: a professional at work.
- **Narrative significance**: Establishes Alexis as the protagonist and immediately codes her as dangerous. The clean, expensive apartment contrasts with the violence she's bringing into it. The audience doesn't yet know whose flat this is or why she's here.
- **Visual note**: A red "X" mark is faintly visible on the lower-left wall — possibly a production mark or a deliberate set-dressing detail suggesting a target.

### Shot 02
- **Timecode**: 00:00:40
- **Scene**: Scene 2 — INT. GARY'S FLAT - DAY
- **Character(s)**: Gary
- **Location**: Gary's flat — living room sofa
- **Framing**: Medium shot, centred. Gary sits on a dark sofa, framed against a warm-toned wall. A Venetian-style mask and what appears to be drug paraphernalia sit on the sofa beside him.
- **Lighting**: Warm, amber domestic light. A single visible cable runs down the wall behind him — the flat feels rough, improvised.
- **Colour palette**: Honey amber, dark brown, cream. The paisley pattern on his shirt adds visual texture.
- **Action**: Gary is seated, looking down with a faint, knowing smile — almost amused by something. He appears relaxed, possibly high. The mask beside him foreshadows the heist.
- **Narrative significance**: Introduces Gary in his natural habitat: stoned, charming, surrounded by the tools of his chaotic life. The mask on the sofa is Chekhov's gun — it will reappear during the burglary of Davina's house and the apartment invasion.
- **Visual note**: A dark poster or artwork is partially visible on the wall behind him, contributing to the cluttered, bohemian atmosphere.

### Shot 03
- **Timecode**: 00:01:21
- **Scene**: Scene 3 — INT. ANGELO'S RESTAURANT BASEMENT - NIGHT
- **Character(s)**: Angelo's associate (left), Angelo (right)
- **Location**: Angelo's restaurant basement
- **Framing**: Medium two-shot. The associate sits with arms folded on the left; Angelo is in profile on the right, his white shirt catching the light. Striped upholstery and graffiti murals fill the background.
- **Lighting**: Warm amber with a slight green cast from the neon signage out of frame. The graffiti-covered walls absorb the light unevenly.
- **Colour palette**: Gold, brown, black, white (Angelo's shirt). The bold graphic murals add pops of colour.
- **Action**: A tense conversation. The associate's folded arms signal resistance or discomfort; Angelo leans in, speaking with gravity. This is the restaurant business — Angelo managing his world.
- **Narrative significance**: Establishes the restaurant as a location where deals are made and debts are owed. Angelo's white shirt visually separates him from the underground world around him — he's trying to stay clean in a dirty business.

### Shot 04
- **Timecode**: 00:01:51
- **Scene**: Scene 3 — INT. ANGELO'S RESTAURANT BASEMENT - NIGHT (continued)
- **Character(s)**: Angelo (back to camera), Hans (seated, background left)
- **Location**: Angelo's restaurant basement — wider establishing angle
- **Framing**: Wide shot. Angelo stands in the centre-right foreground with his back to camera, his white shirt a bright focal point. Hans is visible seated at a table in the background left, partially obscured. The "BASEMENT LIQUOR" neon sign glows green on the right wall. Multiple tables and chairs fill the space.
- **Lighting**: The green neon sign provides a secondary light source that washes the right side of the frame. Warm overhead spots pick out the tables. The graffiti walls are fully visible.
- **Colour palette**: Green (neon), amber (overheads), the black-and-white graphic quality of the murals.
- **Action**: Angelo approaches or addresses someone at a table. The spatial relationship reveals the basement's layout — it's a working restaurant space, not just a back room.
- **Narrative significance**: The first time we see the restaurant in its full geography. The "BASEMENT LIQUOR" sign is both a literal description and a thematic statement — everything in this story happens underground, beneath the surface.

### Shot 05
- **Timecode**: 00:02:38
- **Scene**: Scene 5 — EXT. DAVINA'S HOUSE - NIGHT
- **Character(s)**: Yvonne
- **Location**: Suburban residential street at night
- **Framing**: Medium-long shot, centred. Yvonne walks toward camera down a pavement, framed between garden hedges on the left and a parked van on the right. A streetlight backlights the scene.
- **Lighting**: Sodium streetlight orange, mixed with cool ambient night. The van's plate is partially readable. Hedgerows and garden walls frame the walkway.
- **Colour palette**: Dark green, orange-grey, black. Desaturated, documentary-like.
- **Action**: Yvonne walks alone down the street, wearing a headscarf/bandana and a dark jacket with light trousers. She carries something against her chest. Her posture is purposeful but wary — she's on a delivery.
- **Narrative significance**: Yvonne's introduction places her on the street, in transit, doing Hans's work. The suburban normality of the setting contrasts with the criminal errand. She's the courier, moving between worlds.

### Shot 06
- **Timecode**: 00:03:39
- **Scene**: Scene 6 — EXT. DAVINA'S HOUSE - NIGHT
- **Character(s)**: Alexis
- **Location**: Outside Davina's house — front door area, hedges and white wall visible
- **Framing**: Medium close-up. Alexis stands in three-quarter profile against the foliage of a hedge, a white rendered wall or pillar on the right. Shallow depth of field softens the background.
- **Lighting**: Cool, flat ambient night light with a distant streetlight providing gentle fill. Minimal contrast — her face is softly lit from screen right.
- **Colour palette**: Muted grey-green, cream (wall), olive (jacket).
- **Action**: Alexis waits outside her sister Davina's house, looking off-screen with a pained, conflicted expression. She wears a quilted leather jacket over a light t-shirt. This is personal, not professional — her guard is down.
- **Narrative significance**: The first time we see Alexis outside of a professional context. Standing outside her ill sister's house, unable or unwilling to go in, she's caught between her two lives. The vulnerability in her expression is the emotional core of her character.

### Shot 07
- **Timecode**: 00:04:09
- **Scene**: Scene 7 — INT. GARY'S FLAT - DAY
- **Character(s)**: Yvonne (foreground right), Gary (background left, doorway)
- **Location**: Gary's flat
- **Framing**: Over-the-shoulder / two-plane composition. Yvonne occupies the right foreground in semi-profile, gripping something (a bag, a jacket). Gary stands in a doorway in the mid-ground left, wearing his paisley shirt, watching her. Graffiti or murals are partially visible on the left edge.
- **Lighting**: Flat, warm domestic light. The doorway behind Gary glows with warmer amber from another room.
- **Colour palette**: Warm brown, cream, olive.
- **Action**: Yvonne has arrived at Gary's flat — or is leaving it. The spatial separation between them (she's near the exit, he's deeper inside) suggests tension. She appears to be clutching something protectively.
- **Narrative significance**: This is the intersection of two storylines — Yvonne the courier and Gary the thief. Whatever she's carrying is the MacGuffin that connects Hans's operation to Gary's desperation. The physical distance between them in the frame mirrors their misaligned goals.

### Shot 08
- **Timecode**: 00:04:42
- **Scene**: Scene 8 — INT. ANGELO'S RESTAURANT BASEMENT - NIGHT
- **Character(s)**: Angelo
- **Location**: Angelo's restaurant basement — seated at his position
- **Framing**: Medium close-up, centred. Angelo fills the frame, his white shirt luminous against the dark background. Graffiti murals, a shisha pipe, and the faint glow of the "BASEMENT LIQUOR" neon are visible behind him.
- **Lighting**: A single green-tinged source lights his face from screen left, giving his skin a sickly, underwater quality. The rest of the frame falls into deep shadow.
- **Colour palette**: Green, gold, deep black. The white shirt glows almost radioactive.
- **Action**: Angelo sits alone, staring off-screen with a heavy, troubled expression. His mouth is slightly set. He looks older in this light — burdened.
- **Narrative significance**: A portrait of a man in over his head. The green light — from the "BASEMENT LIQUOR" sign — literally colours him with the taint of his underground dealings. He's trapped in his own restaurant, in his own debt.

### Shot 09
- **Timecode**: 00:05:14
- **Scene**: Scene 9 — INT. CANARY WHARF APARTMENT - NIGHT
- **Character(s)**: Yvonne (silhouette)
- **Location**: Canary Wharf apartment — hallway looking into the living space
- **Framing**: Medium shot from behind. Yvonne is in silhouette on the left side of frame, her arm extended, holding what appears to be a phone or small object. Through the doorway, the apartment's living area opens up: white furniture, a small table, and the floor-to-ceiling window revealing the Docklands skyline at night.
- **Lighting**: The apartment interior provides a cool blue-white backlight. Yvonne is almost entirely in shadow — a dark shape against the illuminated space.
- **Colour palette**: Midnight blue, silver, the warm amber dots of the distant city lights.
- **Action**: Yvonne enters or surveys the apartment, arm raised as if checking something or bracing herself. She's a trespasser in this clean, expensive world.
- **Narrative significance**: The contrast between Yvonne's dark silhouette and the pristine apartment is the film's class divide in a single image. The Docklands skyline through the window is London's wealth, glittering and unreachable. She's standing at the threshold.

### Shot 10
- **Timecode**: 00:05:36
- **Scene**: Scene 10 — INT. HANS'S BASEMENT - NIGHT
- **Character(s)**: Clarence (foreground), bodyguard (background right)
- **Location**: Hans's basement
- **Framing**: Tight close-up. Clarence's face fills the left two-thirds of the frame. His long curly hair falls across his face, partially obscuring one eye. A bodyguard is visible but out of focus behind his right shoulder. Exposed brick and pipes in the background.
- **Lighting**: Harsh, flat overhead light from the bare bulb (out of frame). Deep shadows under Clarence's brow and jawline.
- **Colour palette**: Grey, brown, washed-out skin tones. Deliberately ugly, prison-like.
- **Action**: Clarence stares directly into the lens — or at something just past it. His expression is a mix of defiance and fear. He's been held captive; the rawness in his eyes shows it.
- **Narrative significance**: This is our introduction to the hostage. The same face as Gary but with none of his chaotic energy — Clarence is still, coiled, enduring. The tight framing traps him in the shot the way he's trapped in the basement. The bodyguard behind him is a constant threat.

### Shot 11
- **Timecode**: 00:06:04
- **Scene**: Scene 11 — INT. HANS'S BASEMENT - NIGHT
- **Character(s)**: Clarence (left), Hans (right)
- **Location**: Hans's basement
- **Framing**: Extreme close-up two-shot. Both faces fill the frame in near-profile, inches apart. Hans's hand reaches up to touch or grip Clarence's face. The intimacy of the framing is deliberately uncomfortable.
- **Lighting**: Soft, diffused. The bare bulb is distant or off-axis, leaving both faces in a gentle wash that emphasises the texture of skin, beard, sweat.
- **Colour palette**: Muted flesh tones, grey, the dark of their clothing blending into shadow.
- **Action**: Hans holds Clarence's face — a gesture that's simultaneously paternal and menacing. Clarence's eyes are closed or downcast. Hans is close enough to whisper. This is dominance performed as tenderness.
- **Narrative significance**: The power dynamic between captor and captive distilled into a single image. Hans doesn't need chains or weapons — his control is psychological, physical, intimate. This shot tells you everything about how Hans operates: he owns people by getting close to them.

### Shot 12
- **Timecode**: 00:06:30
- **Scene**: Scene 12 — INT. CANARY WHARF APARTMENT - NIGHT
- **Character(s)**: Gary (foreground, skull mask), Alexis (background, blurred)
- **Location**: Canary Wharf apartment
- **Framing**: Shallow depth of field close-up. In the foreground, the skull mask is in sharp focus — white bone with painted red blood at the teeth and eye sockets. Through the blur behind it, Alexis is visible in the hallway. A hand holds something (possibly rolling a cigarette or manipulating an object).
- **Lighting**: Cool apartment light. The mask's white surface catches and reflects it, appearing almost to glow.
- **Colour palette**: White (mask), grey-blue (apartment), red accents (painted blood on the mask).
- **Action**: The mask sits in the foreground as an object of menace while the apartment scene plays out behind it. This is either a POV from near the mask or a deliberately composed shot that forces the viewer to look through death to see the action.
- **Narrative significance**: The skull mask is the film's most potent visual symbol — it transforms Gary and the bodyguards from people into archetypes, making the violence anonymous and ritualistic. Placing it in sharp focus while the action blurs behind it inverts the usual hierarchy: the symbol matters more than the event.

### Shot 13
- **Timecode**: 00:06:40
- **Scene**: Scene 13 — INT. HANS'S BASEMENT - NIGHT
- **Character(s)**: Hans (seated, left), Clarence (standing, centre), bodyguard (right, back to camera)
- **Location**: Hans's basement
- **Framing**: Wide three-shot. Hans sits on a chair to the left, glass of whisky in hand, legs crossed, relaxed. Clarence stands in the centre holding what appears to be a wooden frame or the easel. A bodyguard sits with his back to camera on the right. The bare lightbulb hangs between them. Exposed brick walls, pipes overhead.
- **Lighting**: The single bare bulb is the dominant source, casting a harsh pool of light in the centre and leaving the edges in shadow. Hans catches some of the spill on the left.
- **Colour palette**: Concrete grey, warm bulb yellow, black clothing.
- **Action**: A tableau of captivity. Hans watches Clarence from a position of comfortable power. Clarence stands — perhaps mid-task, perhaps being made to perform. The bodyguard monitors. The chess game or some form of ritualistic exchange is underway.
- **Narrative significance**: The spatial arrangement tells the story: Hans is seated (power), Clarence stands (servitude), the bodyguard watches (enforcement). The bare bulb is an interrogation-room cliché made literal. Hans has turned this basement into a theatre where he's both audience and director.

### Shot 14
- **Timecode**: 00:07:03
- **Scene**: Scene 14 — INT. DAVINA'S HOUSE - NIGHT (The Burglary)
- **Character(s)**: Two masked figures (Gary and a bodyguard)
- **Location**: Inside Davina's house — kitchen or hallway
- **Framing**: Medium two-shot. Both figures wear skull masks with painted blood details. The figure on the left holds a phone with its torch on, the light flaring directly into the lens. The figure on the right is slightly closer to camera, adjusting their mask or necklace. Blue-tinted moonlight or ambient light fills the space behind them. Domestic details visible: tiled floor, counter edge.
- **Lighting**: The phone torch provides a harsh, directional white light that blows out the centre of the frame. The surrounding space is lit in cold blue.
- **Colour palette**: Ice blue, white (masks and torch), black (clothing).
- **Action**: The burglars are inside the house, using a phone torch to navigate. The masks transform them into horror-movie figures — faceless, skeletal. They're professional enough to mask up but amateur enough to use a phone for light.
- **Narrative significance**: This is where Gary's storyline collides with Alexis's: he's robbing her sister's house. The skull masks turn a domestic burglary into something folkloric, almost Day of the Dead. The blue-and-white palette is the visual opposite of Gary's warm amber flat — he's crossed into someone else's world.

### Shot 15
- **Timecode**: 00:07:20
- **Scene**: Scene 15 — INT. HANS'S BASEMENT - NIGHT
- **Character(s)**: Hans (left), Clarence (right)
- **Location**: Hans's basement
- **Framing**: Tight two-shot, both facing camera at slight angles. Hans grips Clarence's jacket or collar with one hand, pulling him close. Their faces are level, almost touching. Pipes and wiring visible on the ceiling above them.
- **Lighting**: Flat, harsh overhead light. Unflattering on both faces — every pore, every line visible.
- **Colour palette**: Olive-grey, brown, muted flesh tones.
- **Action**: Hans physically holds Clarence — not violently, but possessively. Clarence stares straight ahead, jaw tight, enduring it. Hans is making a point through proximity, through the threat implied by the hand on the collar.
- **Narrative significance**: Another variation on the Hans-Clarence power dynamic. Where Shot 11 was intimate and almost tender, this one is more overtly threatening — the hand on the collar, the grip on the clothing. Hans is demonstrating ownership. Clarence's refusal to look at him is his only remaining act of resistance.

### Shot 16
- **Timecode**: 00:08:21
- **Scene**: Scene 16 — INT. DAVINA'S HOUSE - NIGHT (The Burglary escalates)
- **Character(s)**: Davina (left, hostage), masked figure (right, skull mask)
- **Location**: Inside Davina's house — doorway
- **Framing**: Medium two-shot in a doorway. Davina is on the left, her mouth covered with a surgical mask or tape, one hand raised in surrender. The masked figure behind/beside her wears the skull mask and has an arm around her — controlling her, steering her. They've just entered or are moving through a room.
- **Lighting**: Cold blue-grey domestic light. A light switch or intercom panel is visible on the wall left.
- **Colour palette**: Steel blue, white (mask, wall), black (clothing).
- **Action**: The burglary has become a home invasion. Davina has been found and restrained. The skull mask makes the masked figure nightmarish — a death's-head in a suburban hallway. Davina's raised hand and covered mouth convey terror and helplessness.
- **Narrative significance**: The moment the burglary crosses a line. Finding someone home turns theft into something worse. For the audience who already knows Davina is Alexis's ill sister, the dread is doubled — we know what this will trigger when Alexis finds out. The surgical mask on Davina may also reference her illness.

### Shot 17
- **Timecode**: 00:08:27
- **Scene**: Scene 17 — INT. CANARY WHARF APARTMENT - NIGHT
- **Character(s)**: Gary
- **Location**: Canary Wharf apartment
- **Framing**: Low-angle medium close-up. Gary is shot from below, filling the upper half of the frame. He holds a shotgun, the barrel pointed slightly off-camera. His long hair falls forward, his mouth is open — he's shouting or breathing hard. The white ceiling and wall of the apartment are behind him.
- **Lighting**: Cool, flat apartment lighting. The low angle makes the white ceiling dominant, giving the shot a clinical, exposed quality.
- **Colour palette**: Grey-white, black (clothing), the dark metal of the shotgun.
- **Action**: Gary has the shotgun and has taken control — or is trying to. The low angle grants him temporary power in the frame, but his open mouth and wild expression suggest desperation more than dominance. He's in over his head with a weapon he doesn't fully know how to wield.
- **Narrative significance**: Gary with a gun is the film's most volatile equation. The shotgun is from the heist at Davina's (or has been brought to the apartment); either way, it's a working-class weapon in a luxury flat. The low angle is a visual lie — it makes him look powerful, but the story has already shown us he's not in control.

### Shot 18
- **Timecode**: 00:08:37
- **Scene**: Scene 18 — INT. GARY'S FLAT - DAY/NIGHT
- **Character(s)**: Gary
- **Location**: Gary's flat — kitchenette/bathroom area
- **Framing**: Medium shot through a doorway. Gary is perched on a counter or appliance in the small kitchen/bathroom space, hand to his face, looking distressed. A red office chair is visible in the foreground left. The flat is cramped, cluttered — clothes, towels, equipment visible.
- **Lighting**: Flat, slightly cool light from a window (right side). The space is small enough that it's evenly lit with nowhere to hide.
- **Colour palette**: Off-white, grey, red (chair), muted domestic tones.
- **Action**: Gary sits alone in his cramped flat, head in his hand. He could be coming down, withdrawing, processing what he's done, or preparing for what comes next. The pose is one of exhaustion or despair.
- **Narrative significance**: Strips Gary of the chaotic energy he had on the sofa in Shot 02. Here, the flat looks less bohemian and more like a trap — small, messy, inescapable. The red chair is the only vivid colour in the frame, drawing the eye and adding unease.

### Shot 19
- **Timecode**: 00:09:35
- **Scene**: Scene 19 — INT. CANARY WHARF APARTMENT - NIGHT (The Heist)
- **Character(s)**: Gary or a bodyguard (left, entering), masked figure holding Yvonne hostage (right)
- **Location**: Canary Wharf apartment — main living space
- **Framing**: Wide shot. A figure in black enters from the left through the apartment door, leaning forward in motion. In the centre-right, a skull-masked figure holds Yvonne from behind with an arm around her neck. The floor-to-ceiling window behind them shows the Docklands skyline. White walls, minimal furniture, a coffee table with a bowl.
- **Lighting**: Cool blue-grey apartment light. The city lights through the window provide a secondary warm glow on the right edge.
- **Colour palette**: White (walls), steel blue, black (clothing), amber (distant city lights).
- **Action**: The apartment invasion is in full swing. Yvonne is being used as a human shield or is restrained by one of the masked attackers. Another figure is entering — the situation is escalating, with multiple people now in the space.
- **Narrative significance**: The clean apartment has been violated. The composition is almost theatrical — the wide shot shows us the entire stage: the door (entry point), the hostage (centre), and the window/skyline (the world outside that doesn't know or care). Yvonne as hostage connects Gary's desperation to Hans's operation.

### Shot 20
- **Timecode**: 00:09:46
- **Scene**: Scene 20 — INT. CANARY WHARF APARTMENT - NIGHT
- **Character(s)**: Yvonne (foreground, skull mask on, held at gunpoint), Alexis (behind)
- **Location**: Canary Wharf apartment
- **Framing**: Extreme close-up. The skull mask fills the centre of the frame, its wearer's eyes — wide, terrified — visible through the eye sockets. This is Yvonne, now wearing the mask (or having it held to her face). Behind her, slightly out of focus, Alexis peers around, a pistol or shotgun barrel visible pressing against the mask near the temple. City lights glow in the soft background right.
- **Colour palette**: White (mask), grey, muted red (painted blood on mask teeth), blurred amber (lights).
- **Action**: The mask has been forced onto Yvonne, or she's grabbed it as a shield. Either way, the image is startling — a skull face with living, frightened eyes. Alexis is behind her with a weapon. The hunter has caught up with the prey, but the prey is wearing the predator's face.
- **Narrative significance**: The most striking single image in the film. The skull mask stops being a disguise and becomes a death sentence — whoever wears it is marked. Yvonne's real eyes looking through the mask's hollow sockets collapse the distance between symbol and person. The painted blood on the mask rhymes with the real violence about to happen.

### Shot 21
- **Timecode**: 00:09:51
- **Scene**: Scene 20 — INT. CANARY WHARF APARTMENT - NIGHT (aftermath)
- **Character(s)**: Gary (dead on the floor)
- **Location**: Canary Wharf apartment
- **Framing**: High-angle or floor-level shot. Gary lies on the ground, his face and upper body visible. Blood covers his face and the surface beneath him.
- **Lighting**: Cool apartment light, flat and unforgiving.
- **Colour palette**: Red (blood), grey-white (apartment floor/wall), dark clothing.
- **Action**: Gary is dead. Alexis has shot him — "a bullet in his forehead." He lies exactly where he fell, crumpled and still. The blood is vivid against the clean apartment.
- **Narrative significance**: The consequence of everything Gary set in motion. His death on the pristine apartment floor is the collision of his messy, desperate world with the sterile one he invaded. No dignity, no last words. The violence Alexis carries out professionally is here personal — she knows what he did to her sister.

### Shot 22
- **Timecode**: 00:10:09
- **Scene**: Scene 20 — INT./EXT. CANARY WHARF APARTMENT BALCONY - NIGHT
- **Character(s)**: Alexis
- **Location**: Balcony of the Canary Wharf apartment
- **Framing**: Medium shot. Alexis stands on the balcony, her pistol raised and pointed off-camera right. The Docklands skyline stretches behind her — tower blocks, cranes, city lights reflected on the Thames. Her hair is pulled back, her posture locked.
- **Lighting**: City-light ambient. Warm amber from the skyline backlights her, cool blue on her face from the apartment.
- **Colour palette**: Night blue, amber city glow, dark clothing.
- **Action**: Alexis has followed Yvonne onto the balcony and has her weapon aimed. Yvonne has head-butted her and run. Alexis has the shot — but this is the moment of choice: does she pull the trigger?
- **Narrative significance**: The skyline behind her is the world going on as normal while she decides someone's fate. The raised pistol against the cityscape is iconic — it belongs on the poster. This is the fulcrum of Alexis's story: professional killer vs. human being.

### Shot 23
- **Timecode**: 00:10:12
- **Scene**: Scene 20/21 — EXT. CANARY WHARF APARTMENT BALCONY - NIGHT
- **Character(s)**: Yvonne
- **Location**: Balcony / building exterior
- **Framing**: Low-angle close-up. Yvonne is shot from below, her face filling the upper portion of the frame. Red-tinged light washes across her. The edges of buildings are visible behind her.
- **Lighting**: A warm red cast — possibly from a building sign, emergency light, or colour grading — bathes the entire frame. It feels like a different world from the cool apartment interior.
- **Colour palette**: Deep red, black, warm amber.
- **Action**: Yvonne looks down — at Alexis, at the ground, at her fate. Her expression is raw, emotional. Three seconds after the previous shot. The shift from cool blue (Alexis) to hot red (Yvonne) signals a change in perspective and emotional register.
- **Narrative significance**: The red wash transforms this from thriller into something almost operatic. Yvonne is bathed in the colour of blood, danger, emotion. The low angle puts the audience in Alexis's position — looking up at the person she might kill, seeing a human being, not a target.

### Shot 24
- **Timecode**: 00:11:46
